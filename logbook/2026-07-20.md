Update — GDELT collection: rate limiting and query relevance

[RUBRIC: Technical/Practical Effectiveness — constraints and limitations]


GDELT enforces a stricter rate limit than documented (courtesy message
states "one request per 5 seconds"; in practice, 6s and even 15s
between requests still triggered 429 Too Many Requests errors on
multiple queries). Retry logic with backoff implemented; some queries
still required multiple attempts to succeed.
Same lesson as the earlier Guardian API issue: unquoted multi-word
queries triggered loose/fuzzy matching rather than exact phrase
matching, returning irrelevant results (e.g. a Groton, NY high school
graduation article, a Lewis Hamilton F1 article) alongside genuine
hits. Fixed using quoted-phrase syntax.
Manually verified relevance of each returned article rather than
trusting raw counts (same discipline applied to the Guardian API
results). Findings:

"Ready or Not, AI Government is Already Here" (naked capitalism,
syndicated on slguardian.org) — CONFIRMED relevant: substantive
Diella coverage (OpenAI/Microsoft infrastructure, transparency
concerns quoted from an Albanian IT expert), though framed as one
example within a broader global AI-governance opinion piece, not a
dedicated Diella news article. Useful as evidence of international
citation of the Diella case study.
"In an Albanian Graft Case, Fake Buyers, Friendly Rates, Cash in
Crates" (Balkan Insight) — mentions AKSHI, not Diella; relevant as
AKSHI-corruption context, matched correctly on the "AKSHI" query.
"Prime Minister Rama: By 2030, the new public procurement system
will use AI" (gazetaexpress.com) — link returns 404 as of manual
check (2026-07-20), approximately 2 months after publication
(seendate 2026-05-11). Noted as a minor data-quality/link-rot
observation for the report. Checked Wayback Machine — no archived
snapshot exists either. Article content is not recoverable; excluded
from the usable corpus. Title and seendate retained in raw GDELT
metadata as evidence the article existed and was indexed, but cannot
be cited directly or verified further.
Two additional Rama/Albania-context articles (Seoul visit, EU
accession) not yet assessed for direct relevance — flagged for
follow-up.
